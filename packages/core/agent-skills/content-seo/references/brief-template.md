# Brief template

Use this structure for every brief saved with create_artifact (kind brief). Translate the headings into the conversation's language (pt-BR headings in parentheses below). Keep each section short and factual. A value you did not measure says "not measured" ("não medido"), never 0 and never a guess.

The brief separates three things: what was measured (with the tool or page it came from), what you concluded, and what the person decided. Never present a conclusion as a measurement.

## Title

`Brief: <working title>` (pt-BR: `Brief: <título provisório>`).

## 1. Summary (Resumo)

One paragraph: what the piece is, for whom, why now, and the angle that makes it different from what already ranks.

## 2. Target (Alvo)

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Primary keyword | keyword, monthly volume or "not measured" |
| Variations | up to 10, each with volume or "not measured" |
| Market | country and language from the project |
| Content type | article, guide, landing page, refresh of an existing URL |
| Search intent | informational, commercial investigation, transactional or navigational, plus one line of evidence |
| Goal and call to action | what the reader should do next |
| Audience | who reads it and what they already know |

## 3. Evidence (Evidências)

Google results (from search_results): a Top 3 table with position, URL, page type, word count, angle, proof shown and what is missing. Below it, one line each for SERP features present, People also ask questions (verbatim), and AI Overview sources (domains).

AI answers (from Lastfind data): the prompts closest to the topic, which brands AI answers mention for them, whether and how they mention this brand, the page types they cite, the fan-out searches, and the attributes AIs associate with the brand versus competitors. Name the date range when the data shows one.

Brand's existing content: pages on the topic found in the results, in ranked_keywords or given by the person. If the brand already ranks with a page for the primary keyword, say so and recommend a refresh instead of a new page; the person decides.

## 4. Market consensus (Consenso do mercado)

Recurring definitions. Frameworks repeated in at least three independent sources (name them). The most cited numbers, each with its source. Omissions and angles rarely covered. Standard jargon and common pitfalls. Quotations are marked as quotations; your synthesis is marked as synthesis.

## 5. Brand point of view (Ponto de vista da marca)

Two separate blocks. Declared: what the brand says about itself and its positions, from its pages (title and URL) or from the person. Inferred: positions you deduce from institutional pages or adjacent posts, each labeled inferred. Then: where the brand diverges from consensus, and gaps it has not addressed.

Voice: the rules or example text the person gave. If none, three to five observed voice traits from the brand's pages, labeled inferred.

## 6. Target length (Tamanho-alvo)

Articles: highest valid Top 3 word count × 1.2, floor 2,000 words, rounded up to the next 100. Show the basis, for example "Top 3 highest: 2,350 words; × 1.2 = 2,820; target 2,900". If fewer than two Top 3 pages were measured, write "not measured; the outline sets the length" and say why.

Landing pages: no floor. Give the range of the Top 3 landing pages and the length the offer needs.

## 7. Outline (Estrutura)

H1, then each H2 and H3 with:

- must cover: the points the section has to make;
- label: follow consensus, diverge with brand point of view, or add unique value (say what the unique value is);
- questions answered: the People also ask and fan-out questions mapped to it.

Every mapped question must land in some section. A section labeled add unique value must contain something the Top 3 lack (data the brand owns, first-hand process, a comparison nobody made, a clearer definition).

### Landing-page variant

Replace the H2 outline with blocks, each with its purpose and the proof it may use: hero (headline, subheadline, primary call to action); problem; how it works; benefits, each tied to proof; social proof, only from confirmed sources; objections and FAQ, from People also ask and AI answers; plan or pricing information only if the person provides it; final call to action.

## 8. Terms (Termos)

Required: brand vocabulary, product names, entities the piece must name (brand, category, country). Banned: clichés repeated across the Top 3, plus the words to avoid in writing-quality.md.

## 9. E-E-A-T inputs (Sinais de E-E-A-T)

Author: name and credentials, confirmed by the person, or an open item. First-hand experience to include: examples, process, data the brand owns, or an open item. Proof you can use: claim and source. Do not claim: anything unconfirmed. For YMYL topics (health, money, legal, safety), a qualified author or reviewer is required before publishing.

## 10. On-page plan (SEO na página)

Two or three title tag options (about 60 characters, keyword near the start), one meta description (about 155 characters), slug, suggested schema type, internal links (only URLs confirmed by a tool or the person; otherwise list them as link opportunities to fill), external sources to cite, image ideas.

## 11. AI answer plan (Plano para respostas de IA)

The Lastfind prompts this piece should help win, or new buyer questions to propose with create_prompts after publication. Answer-first blocks to include. Comparison tables. Off-page notes: when AI answers mostly cite page types the brand cannot publish itself (reviews, listicles, forums), say the piece alone will not change that and name the type.

## 12. Capacity check (Checagem de capacidade)

One line: does the outline reach the target length without filler? If not, what was added or what shorter target you propose and why.

## 13. Limitations and not measured (Limitações)

Everything missing: pages that could not be read, metrics not measured, evidence skipped at the person's request, voice not provided.

## 14. Decision needed (Decisão)

What the person must approve or answer before drafting, as a short numbered list.
