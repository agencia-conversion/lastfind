# Review checklist

Run this on every draft before handing it over, and on any text the person asks you to review. Record each item as pass, fail (with the quoted sentence) or not applicable. Unknown stays unknown: if you cannot verify an item, say so; never mark it as passed.

## Blocking (fix before the person sees the draft)

1. No invented or unsourced number, statistic, quote, study, client, award, credential, ranking or result.
2. Every brand claim about itself is on the "proof you can use" list or was confirmed by the person in this conversation.
3. Written in the requested language, with correct diacritics everywhere (see writing-quality.md).
4. No process language in the body (brief, agent, Lastfind, tool names, search result mechanics) unless the topic needs it.
5. No links to competitor pages that were only studied, no non-public or local URLs, no "sources consulted" section.
6. No placeholders such as "[insert data]" or "XX%" in the body.
7. No em dash in prose and no middle dot, bullet, vertical bar or dash used as a separator.
8. YMYL topics (health, money, legal, safety): a qualified author or reviewer is named, or publishing is flagged as blocked until one is.

## Fix before publishing

### Intent and substance

9. The format matches the dominant intent in the Top 10 (guide, list, comparison, landing page).
10. The first paragraph answers the search: what, for whom, why it matters.
11. Every section of the approved outline is present and covers its "must cover" points.
12. Sections labeled add unique value contain something the Top 3 lack; sections labeled diverge with brand point of view state the brand's position clearly.
13. Every People also ask and fan-out question mapped in the brief is answered in the first one or two sentences of its section.
14. Length reaches the brief's target without filler. If it falls short, add substance from the brief; if the outline cannot support it, tell the person.

### SEO

15. Primary keyword in the title tag, H1, first 100 words, at least one H2 and the meta description, with no stuffing.
16. Required terms present; banned terms (Top 3 clichés) absent.
17. One H1; no skipped heading levels; no heading directly after another heading or a list.
18. Articles: at most three bulleted items in total, unless the person asked for lists.
19. Publication fields complete in the content's Overview (set_content_overview, never in the text): title tag (about 60 characters), meta description (about 155 characters), slug (ASCII, short), schema suggestion, internal links (confirmed URLs or listed as opportunities), image ideas with alt text, open items.

### Writing quality

20. No word or phrase from the avoid lists in writing-quality.md; promotional adjectives only with proof next to them.
21. No source description ("the website says"); facts stated and attributed by name.
22. Each number and quote names its source in the sentence and links the original public page with a descriptive anchor.
23. Short sentences and paragraphs; one idea per paragraph.

### E-E-A-T and AI answers

24. Author named with real credentials, or listed as an open item.
25. First-hand experience (examples, process, the brand's own data) present where the brief asked for it, or listed as an open item.
26. Answer-first passages under question headings, self-contained when read alone.
27. Brand, category, audience and country named explicitly; brand and product names consistent.
28. Comparisons shown as tables when the intent is comparison.

## Nice to have

29. A visible updated date in the publishing notes.
30. At least one element the brand owns (a diagram, a checklist, data, a worked example).
31. A clear next step for the reader that matches the goal in the brief.

## How to report

To the person, in a few lines: "Checked 31 items: 27 passed, 3 fixed, 1 needs you", then the items that need them, numbered, one line each.

When reviewing the person's own text, save or show a table:

| Item | Result | Evidence | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20. Words to avoid | fail | "Neste artigo, vamos entender..." | Open with the answer: "Um CRM para pequenas empresas deve..." |

A failed blocking item means the text is not ready to publish. Say so plainly. Do not silently rewrite the person's text; propose the fix and ask.

If you notice a recurring problem that is specific to this brand (a word their drafts keep using, a claim they keep making without proof), point it out once and ask whether to treat it as a rule for the rest of the conversation.
